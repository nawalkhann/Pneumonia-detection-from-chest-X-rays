Pneumonia detection from chest X-rays

A deep learning model that reads pediatric chest X-rays and flags pneumonia. It was fine-tuned from an ImageNet-pretrained CNN, tuned to catch as many sick patients as possible, and explained with Grad-CAM heatmaps.

What this project does

Pneumonia is one of the leading causes of death in children under five, and a chest X-ray is the standard way to confirm it. Reading X-rays takes trained radiologists, who are in short supply in many places. A screening model that flags likely pneumonia can put the most urgent images at the top of the queue.

This project trains such a model on the public Kaggle Chest X-Ray Images (Pneumonia) dataset: 5,856 real X-rays of children aged one to five from Guangzhou Women and Children's Medical Center, labeled by expert physicians.

  • Transfer learning. An EfficientNet-B0 network pretrained on ImageNet is fine-tuned end to end on the X-rays. Starting from general image features is far faster and more accurate than training from scratch on six thousand images.
  • Optimized for recall. Missing a sick patient is much worse than a false alarm, so the decision threshold is chosen on validation data to keep recall at 98% or more. On the test set the model misses only 4 of 390 pneumonia cases.
  • Explainable. Grad-CAM heatmaps show which part of each X-ray drove the prediction, so you can check whether the model is looking at the lungs or at something it should not.

Tech stack

Python, PyTorch, timm (EfficientNet-B0), scikit-learn for metrics, matplotlib for figures, and a hand-written Grad-CAM using PyTorch hooks. Training ran on a 4-core CPU in about 30 minutes.

Not a medical device. This is a portfolio and learning project trained on a single-site pediatric dataset. It should not be used to diagnose anyone.
